## For teachers

Put The Invisible Trail on the classroom screen. Ask the class to predict what a click might leave behind before turning on the Privacy Goggles. Spend 15 minutes testing the five missions and 15 minutes redrawing one data trail on paper. Finish by asking students to show who receives one record and which choice changes its destination. ## Media description

MetaPet School is an independent educational initiative developed in Frankston by Blue $nake Studio. Its teacher-led Years 3–6 offering includes seven classroom sessions and an optional digital privacy activity, The Invisible Trail. Students work with fictional data to investigate activity records, permissions, predictions and data-sharing choices. Teachers can use one shared screen or a printable alternative.

## Partnership overview

**Purpose**
Help primary students explain how digital records are created, shared and interpreted, and how a person can change those outcomes.

**What a school can review**
Seven existing teacher-led sessions, the optional 30-minute lab, the print pathway, parent information and the local-data controls.

**Proposed educator evaluation**
Invite an educator to review the guide and rehearse the lab. If the school chooses to trial it, use its approved supervision and consent processes. Have the educator assess whether students can link an action to a record, question a prediction and justify a design choice. The school can share aggregate feedback on timing, clarity and accessibility. Keep any student work within the school.

**Contact**
Use the contact pathway in the [safeguarding materials](https://www.metapet.school/schools/safeguarding).

## School FAQ

**Which years?** Years 3–6. Younger groups can work with a single record. Older groups can follow multiple copies and explain trade-offs.

**Does the lab replace any lessons?** The seven-session sequence continues as the core programme. The lab is an optional companion.

**What does the lab ask students to enter?** Students choose from fictional examples. The activity has no name or free-text field and requires no microphone, contacts, camera or location access.

**What stays on the device?** Lab choices are held in page memory and clear when reset or reloaded. Other classroom features can keep local records. Teachers can use the school's local-data controls to clear covered records on that browser. Existing exports are separate files.

**What does the host receive?** The host processes ordinary requests needed to deliver the page. Review the school privacy information for the broader classroom data lifecycle.

**Do we need individual devices?** One teacher screen is enough. The guide also supports a fully paper-based lesson.

**Has it been independently evaluated?** The project materials establish a classroom pathway and propose educator evaluation. No independent results are claimed.

**How much does it cost?** The complete Field Mode is free. Contributions are optional.
