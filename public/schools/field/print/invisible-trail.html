The Invisible Trail — Teacher Guide

Years 3–6 · 30 minutes · Optional companion to the seven classroom sessions

Use one teacher screen for 15 minutes of interactive learning, then spend 15 minutes discussing and redrawing one example. For a fully unplugged lesson, run the five missions below on paper before the discussion.

Materials. Paper, pencils and five slips per pair. Print or save this page from your browser.

Five paper missions

  1. Footprint Forest. Write “shape puzzle opened” after a pretend tap. Ask what caused the record and who could see it.
  2. Permission Gates. Sort tile taps, microphone and saved progress into needed, unnecessary and optional. Ask students to justify each choice.
  3. Mirror Market. Pass a “played rhythm garden” slip to a pretend advertiser only when sharing is on. Ask what they could guess and why the guess might be wrong.
  4. Shadow Profile. Combine “plays rhythm levels” and “usually plays at 6 pm”. Ask what changes if a second player uses the device. Listen for the difference between evidence and a guess.
  5. Data Defender. Draw a data trail for an app that saves progress or starts fresh. Cross out unnecessary transfers, mark where the player can erase a record, and ask who can still see copies already shared.

Discussion and assessment

Years 3–4 can sort and explain aloud using one record at a time. Years 5–6 can trace a second copy and explain a trade-off.

Exit prompt. Draw or describe one piece of data, who could receive it and a choice that changes where it goes.

Use fictional examples throughout. Avoid asking students about their actual apps, locations, health, family accounts or online experiences.

Curriculum connections. AC9TDI4P09 and VC2TDI4D05 cover identifying stored or shared information and discussing risks. AC9TDI6P10 and VC2TDI6D06 cover how digital footprints form and persist, and privacy when collecting data.
